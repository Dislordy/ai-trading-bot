What do these numbers mean?
Account value
Everything the paper account is worth right now: cash plus the current value of any SPY shares. It started at $100,000 of fake money.
Total profit/loss
How much the account has gained or lost since the very first run, in dollars and as a percentage.
Today / profit/loss on a date
The change since the run before. Because the bot holds only a small slice of SPY most days, these moves are usually tiny.
Just holding SPY
The benchmark. What $100,000 would be worth if it had bought SPY (an index fund that tracks the 500 biggest US companies) on day one and never touched it. The bot "wins" only if it beats this.
Win rate
Out of the trades that are finished (bought, then later sold), how many made money. Trades still open don't count yet.
Biggest drop from a peak
Also called "max drawdown". The worst fall from a high point to a later low. It shows how painful the bumpiest stretch was.

How the bot decides, every weekday

  1. Download pricesGets years of daily SPY prices and trading volume from Alpaca.
  2. Random Forest guessA machine learning model, trained on past patterns (momentum, trends, volatility), estimates the chance SPY goes up tomorrow.
  3. Claude decidesClaude reads the last 60 days plus that estimate, and picks a small base position or a bigger bet. It explains why in plain English.
  4. Code enforces limitsThe bot places the order on the paper account. Big bets get an automatic stop-loss, so one bet can never lose more than $1,000.

How is this range worked out?
The width comes from volatility
The bot measures how big SPY's daily moves have been over the last 60 trading days. When the market is jumpy, the range is wide. When it's calm, the range is narrow. The range widens the further ahead you look, because uncertainty adds up day after day.
The tilt comes from the AI
The Random Forest only predicts one day ahead. If it leans up, the middle line is nudged up by about one typical day's move times how confident it is. Near 50% means no tilt at all.
Why it's a range, not a line
Nobody can predict exact prices. About half the time SPY should end up inside the darker band, and about 9 times in 10 inside the lighter band. Big news can still push it outside.

Every decision

Newest first. Each morning Claude decides between a small base position (about 5% of the account) and a big bet (up to $20,000, protected by a stop-loss that caps the loss at $1,000). "RF hint" is the Random Forest's estimated chance that SPY goes up, which Claude sees as one input. Above 50% means it leans up, below 50% means it leans down. BUY = added SPY shares, SELL = sold shares, HOLD = kept the position the same. Under each decision is Claude's own explanation.

Paper trading on Alpaca with $100,000 of simulated money. No real money is involved and nothing here is investment advice. Claude reads 60 days of SPY prices plus a Random Forest model's estimate, then picks how much SPY to hold. The code enforces the limits: about 5% normally, at most $20,000 on a big bet, and a stop-loss so a big bet can never lose more than $1,000. Win rate counts completed buy-then-sell trades, using the SPY price when each decision was made.
